Paid Pokpok reports evaluate your brand across 17 markers organised into four categories. Each marker compares what your website communicates versus what your product page communicates. The analysis surfaces alignment gaps, crowded territories, and the specific strategic repositioning moves most likely to differentiate you from your category.

### Foundation Markers

| Marker | What It Measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Purpose** | The reason your brand exists beyond profit — the problem it's in business to solve |
| **Mission & Vision** | The near-term mission (what you're doing now) and long-term vision (what you're building toward) |
| **Values** | The principles that guide decisions — are they specific and behavioural, or generic and decorative? |
| **Positioning** | Where you sit in the category relative to competitors — your claimed territory |

### Identity Markers

| Marker | What It Measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Personality** | The human traits your brand consistently expresses — bold, warm, irreverent, precise, etc. |
| **Archetypes Mix** | The combination of brand archetypes your identity draws on (e.g. Hero + Sage, Rebel + Creator) |
| **Tone of Voice** | How you say things — the register, rhythm, and vocabulary that makes your copy recognisably yours |
| **Visual Identity** | Whether your visual language (colour, typography, imagery direction) reinforces or contradicts your verbal identity |

### Communication Markers

| Marker | What It Measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Messaging** | The core claims your brand makes — are they distinct, specific, and consistent across pages? |
| **Narrative** | The story structure your brand uses — transformation, origin, category creation, etc. |
| **Key Selling Points** | The ranked reasons to choose you — are they functional, emotional, or both? |
| **USP** | The single thing only you can credibly claim in your category |
| **Focus** | Whether your content is tightly focused on one core idea or scattered across multiple competing themes |

### Benefits Markers

| Marker | What It Measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Functional Benefits** | The tangible, practical outcomes your product delivers |
| **Emotional Benefits** | How your customer feels after using your product — confident, calm, seen, powerful, etc. |
| **Self-Expression** | What using your brand says about the customer to themselves |
| **Social Benefits** | What using your brand signals to others — status, belonging, values alignment, etc. |

## Category Saturation

The same 17 markers are used to map every brand across the 15 leading players in your category. For each marker, Pokpok calculates a **Category Saturation Score** (0–100) that reflects how many of those 15 leaders are occupying the same territory.

A saturation score of **65/100** for a marker like Brand Mission means roughly two-thirds of your category competitors are claiming the same mission territory. Positioning yourself there means your brand fades into the category wallpaper.

**How to read saturation:**

- **High saturation (70–100):** This territory is crowded. Occupying it without a distinctly superior execution is a strategic liability.
- **Medium saturation (40–69):** There's room here, but watch competitors gaining ground.
- **Low saturation (0–39):** This is a strategic opportunity. Few or no leaders have claimed this territory, and owning it creates a genuine point of difference.

Use saturation scores to prioritise your repositioning work. The highest-value moves are almost always in low-saturation territories that are also authentic to your brand.

## The 5 Uncomfortable Truths

Each paid Pokpok report distils the full 17-marker analysis into **5 specific, actionable strategic observations** — direct, named findings about where your brand is failing to differentiate, what the category is doing that you're mirroring, and where the real opportunity lies.

These aren't soft recommendations. They're precise provocations designed to reframe the conversation inside your team.

<Accordion title="What does an Uncomfortable Truth look like?">
Here's a real example from a moisturiser category analysis:

**"Everyone Heals, No One Transforms"**

Every major brand in the moisturiser category is communicating healing and restoration as their core emotional benefit. The territory is 94% saturated. Brands that pivot their emotional narrative toward transformation — from damaged to radiant, from tired to powerful — occupy a near-empty territory with no dominant player. The first brand to own it credibly wins it by default.

Each Uncomfortable Truth in your report follows this structure: the observation, the saturation evidence behind it, and the strategic implication.
</Accordion>

<Note>
Markers are evaluated using both your **website (WWW)** and your **product detail page (PDP)**. A gap between the two is called a **Multiple Personality Disorder** finding — your brand is telling one story to browsers and a different story to buyers. This is one of the most common (and most fixable) problems Pokpok surfaces.
</Note>
